An in‑depth look at Ember & Oak’s coffee lineup

September 2026 · 6 min read

At Ember & Oak we believe that a great cup of coffee starts long before the first sip – it begins in the soil, the farmer’s hands, and the careful decisions we make in the roastery. Over the past three years we have curated a lineup that showcases the diversity of the world’s best specialty beans while staying true to our core values: transparency, sustainability, and flavor‑first roasting.

The philosophy behind our lineup

Each coffee in our catalogue is selected based on three criteria:

  1. Origin story. We work directly with small‑holder cooperatives that practice shade‑grown, organic, or regenerative farming. This ensures biodiversity and fair wages.
  2. Processing method. From washed to natural to honey, the way a bean is processed dramatically shapes its flavor. We aim to offer a balanced representation of each method.
  3. Roast profile. Our roasters develop each lot individually, listening to cupping notes and adjusting temperature curves to highlight the bean’s natural strengths without masking its character.

Our coffee portfolio

Ethiopia Yirgacheffe – Bright and floral

Origin: Sidamo, Ethiopia (2025 harvest)
Processing: Washed
Roast: Light (Cupping Score 92)
Tasting notes: Lemon zest, jasmine, and a whisper of honey. A clean, tea‑like finish makes it perfect for pour‑over or Aeropress.

Kenya Kiambu – Vibrant acidity

Origin: Kiambu County, Kenya (2024 harvest)
Processing: Washed
Roast: Light‑medium (Cupping Score 90)
Tasting notes: Blackcurrant, bright citrus, and a wine‑like acidity that lingers. Ideal for a classic V60 brew.

Guatemala Antigua – Balanced sweetness

Origin: Antigua, Guatemala (2025 harvest)
Processing: Natural
Roast: Medium (Cupping Score 91)
Tasting notes: Dark chocolate, caramel, and a hint of spice. The natural process adds a subtle fruit body that shines in a French press.

Colombia Supremo – Smooth and mellow

Origin: Huila, Colombia (2024 harvest)
Processing: Washed
Roast: Medium‑light (Cupping Score 89)
Tasting notes: Red apple, toasted almond, and a silky mouthfeel. A versatile everyday coffee that works well in both drip and espresso.

Sumatra Mandheling – Deep and earthy

Origin: West Sumatra, Indonesia (2023 harvest)
Processing: Semi‑wet (Giling Basah)
Roast: Dark (Cupping Score 88)
Tasting notes: Earth, cedar, and low‑key dark chocolate with a syrupy body. Best enjoyed as a straight espresso or a cold brew for a bold, lingering finish.

Brazil Sul de Minas – Nutty and creamy

Origin: Minas Gerais, Brazil (2025 harvest)
Processing: Natural
Roast: Medium‑dark (Cupping Score 90)
Tasting notes: Hazelnut, toasted coconut, and a buttery mouthfeel. Its low acidity makes it a solid base for milk‑based drinks like latte or flat white.

Roasting technique

Our roasters use a two‑stage profile for most beans:

  • Development phase. After the first crack, we slow the heat to allow sugars to caramelize without burning, preserving the delicate fruit notes in lighter roasts.
  • Finish. For darker roasts we extend the development slightly, letting the bean’s natural oils surface while keeping the acidity in check.

Every batch is cupped three times: pre‑roast, post‑roast, and after a 48‑hour rest. This ensures consistency and gives us the data to fine‑tune the next roast curve.

Sustainability & impact

All of our coffees are sourced under direct trade agreements. Over the past year we have:

  • Invested $45,000 in shade‑tree planting across three farms.
  • Supported 12 women‑led cooperatives with fair‑price premiums.
  • Reduced our carbon footprint by 18 % through solar‑powered roasting.

When you purchase a bag, you receive a QR code that links to a micro‑site detailing the farm, farmer, and exact processing steps for that lot – complete transparency from seed to cup.

Conclusion

Ember & Oak’s lineup is a curated journey through the world’s most expressive coffee regions. Whether you crave the bright citrus of Kenya, the earthy depth of Sumatra, or the creamy comfort of Brazil, there’s a bean that matches your mood and brewing ritual. We invite you to explore each origin, experiment with the suggested methods, and discover how subtle changes in grind, water, and temperature can unlock new layers of flavor.
